# Protect the portfolio performance budget

The Standard Portfolio and Journey Atlas will load no 3D code or generated world package. Hover, focus, touch-down, browser-idle time, navigation to `/journey`, and opening an Atlas entry do not prefetch the 3D payload; World Exploration begins loading only after an explicit Visitor choice. The Portfolio World will target 60 FPS on desktop and at least 30 FPS on mid-range mobile, keep the first playable transfer near or below 2 MB beyond shared portfolio code, adapt shadows, scenery density, and render resolution to device capability, and support both mobile orientations without forcing rotation. Optimization must preserve the Journey Experience Contract: its professional access, content, controls, navigation behavior, and interaction outcomes remain stable even when rendering quality adapts.

Performance changes are accepted against a repeatable 30-second route covering manual movement, camera rotation, Assisted Travel, and a Landmark Encounter. Desktop targets 60 FPS with p95 frame time at or below 20 ms; representative mid-range mobile in low-quality mode must sustain at least 30 FPS with p95 frame time at or below 33.3 ms. An automated Journey regression suite must guard the Journey Experience Contract with desktop and mobile baseline screenshots, interaction checks, and critical-content checks across Adaptive Render Tiers. The first-playable transfer remains subject to the approximately 2 MB budget. The team will not accept a theoretical complexity improvement without a measured benefit on this workload.

An Adaptive Render Tier begins from device capability and may change during the Journey when sustained runtime measurements justify it. Downgrades require sustained frame-budget misses, upgrades require a longer stable period, and cooldowns prevent oscillation. A tier may adjust shadow cost, pixel ratio, and decorative scenery density, but Landmarks, access routes, checkpoints, labels, guidance, and interaction cues remain present.

Production sessions may report coarse performance outcomes through the existing analytics path: initial and final Adaptive Render Tier, a first-playable duration bucket, a frame-time p95 bucket, and the number of quality downgrades. Detailed frame streams remain development-only, and telemetry excludes player coordinates, exact hardware details, and new fingerprinting inputs.

Movement stability is part of the performance budget. V2 advances the Montasim Avatar through bounded kinematic substeps against compiled collision polygons and grounds the Avatar and camera through the compiled terrain-height field and its runtime sampler. Assisted Travel advances an index through a precomputed, sufficiently dense sidewalk and building-access route; it must not recalculate or copy the remaining route each frame, cut diagonally through plots or scenery, or retain destination guidance after arrival. Standard Portfolio overlays and third-party widgets do not mount during World Exploration because they are outside the Journey Experience Contract and compete with the 3D render loop.

The automated frame-budget cases enforce their numeric thresholds only when the browser reports GPU-backed WebGL. Software rasterizers such as SwiftShader are recorded and reported as non-representative rather than treated as evidence for or against the desktop and mobile hardware budgets; interaction, route, content, and visual regressions remain mandatory in that environment.

## Amendment — 2026-07-24

The Standard Portfolio still loads and prefetches no 3D payload. After navigation to `/journey`, a capability check now starts World Exploration and its payload automatically instead of waiting for an Atlas-first chooser. If that check fails, the renderer-independent Journey Atlas opens without requesting the world payload. The transfer, frame-time, adaptation, accessibility, and Journey Experience Contract budgets above remain unchanged.
