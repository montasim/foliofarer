# Adopt the Monsoon Afternoon setting

The Portfolio World will replace the generic green town with a warm, illustrated Bangladeshi Monsoon Afternoon inspired by the atmosphere and restraint of Summer Afternoon without copying its proprietary geometry, textures, branding, or interaction identity. A code-authored coast, river, ponds, island, bridges, terrain, vegetation, houses, civic-use architecture, sky and infrastructure are Environmental Scenery only. Rivers and ponds must read as incised water below a visible dry bank rather than colour laid over level ground; broad lowlands and rises may shape the mainland while compiler-smoothed roads, bridges, arrival pads and access surfaces retain traversal authority. Rocks use deliberately separated small, medium and large silhouettes, with the larger forms held away from travel and interaction corridors. Landmarks remain semantically independent spatial checkpoints, but a neutral Environmental Scenery building may frame each Arrival Forecourt without owning a Portfolio Record, professional copy, an unlock, or an interaction requirement. “Neutral” limits semantic ownership; it does not require every building to share one anonymous residential form. Environmental buildings may use distinct, climate-responsive school, college, office, clinic, library, workshop, hall, and pavilion silhouettes for believable place-making and visual wayfinding. An arrival building may carry one small, façade-mounted Arrival Sign containing only the adjacent Landmark's canonical `shortTitle` for wayfinding. This exception does not make the building own or contain professional evidence, imply an enterable portfolio interior, create a Physical Visit, or control content access. Environmental buildings and Arrival Signs must not display logos, project imagery, professional claims, descriptions, or content state. Professional meaning and authoritative interaction remain in the DOM Journey Atlas, Place Card, Landmark Encounters and Journey Passport. Believable spatial relationships, local silhouettes, colour blocking and the portfolio's passport-ink reveal take priority over photorealism.
